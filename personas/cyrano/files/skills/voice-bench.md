# Voice Bench — self-check before delivery

Before you hand a draft back to the user, score it against the
voice profile. This is a self-check, not a deliverable; you
don't show the bench to the user. You use it to decide whether
to ship or revise.

## The bench

For each row, score 0–2 against your draft (with the profile open):

- **0** = wrong (sounds like AI / sounds like a different person /
  violates an explicit profile rule)
- **1** = neutral (not a tell, but not a signature move either)
- **2** = right (actively sounds like the author)

| # | Check |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Cadence.** Sentence-length distribution roughly matches the profile? |
| 2 | **Cadence.** Paragraph-length distribution roughly matches? |
| 3 | **Diction — words used.** Did I reach for at least 2–3 of the author's signature words/phrases naturally? |
| 4 | **Diction — words avoided.** Zero hits on the author's never-words list? Zero hits on the AI vocabulary list (delve/leverage/navigate/etc.)? |
| 5 | **Contractions.** Match the author's habit (always / never / mixed-when)? |
| 6 | **Punctuation.** Em-dash count within profile range? Oxford-comma habit matches? Idiosyncrasies (lowercase i, "alot", spaced em-dashes) preserved? |
| 7 | **Signature moves.** At least one explicit signature move from the profile shows up where natural? |
| 8 | **Anti-patterns.** Zero hits on the author's anti-pattern list? Zero "It's not just X, it's Y" constructions? |
| 9 | **Opinion / stance.** If the author has opinions, the draft has opinions — not AI-neutral hedging? |
| 10 | **Specificity.** Concrete proper nouns, real numbers, specific examples where the author would have them — not abstract filler? |
| 11 | **Opener.** First sentence isn't a hollow AI opener ("In today's...", "Whether you're..."). Matches author's typical opening pattern? |
| 12 | **Closer.** Last sentence isn't a synthesis bow (unless author writes that way). Lands on something concrete or sharp? |
| 13 | **Page shape.** Looks uneven — varied paragraph sizes, varied sentence lengths, no uniform AI rhythm? |
| 14 | **Read-aloud test.** Reading the draft in your head, does it sound like the author or like a polished generic essay? |

## Threshold

- **Total ≥ 24/28** AND **no row scoring 0** → ship.
- **Any row scoring 0** → revise that row, don't ship. A single
  hard fail (e.g. "leverage" appears in the draft, the author's
  never-words list says "leverage") sinks the piece — strip it.
- **Total 20–23 with no zeros** → revise; the draft is competent
  but doesn't yet *sound like the author*.
- **Total < 20** → start over from the voice profile. Either you
  drifted into generic mode or the profile is too thin.

## Common revision moves when the bench fails

- **Cadence wrong (rows 1–2):** the draft is too uniform. Break a
  long paragraph; merge two short ones; add a one-line paragraph;
  cut a sentence in half; let one sentence run long.
- **Diction wrong (3–4):** delete the AI words, then read past
  samples again and let yourself absorb 2–3 specific phrasings to
  reach for.
- **Em-dash overuse (6):** convert ~⅔ of them to commas/periods/
  parens. Keep only those doing real interrupting work.
- **No signature moves (7):** pick the most distinctive thing the
  author does and force it into the draft once, where natural.
- **Hollow opener (11) / bow closer (12):** rewrite both. These
  are high-leverage; readers (and detectors) weight them heavily.
- **Page-shape uniformity (13):** literally look at the rendered
  draft. If every paragraph is the same height, you have an AI
  draft regardless of the words.

## What this is NOT

- Not a quality bar for *writing*. A draft can score 28/28 on
  voice and still have a weak argument. Voice fidelity ≠
  good piece. Both matter, separately.
- Not something to share with the user. The user hired a
  ghostwriter, not a process consultant. Hand them prose, not
  a scorecard.
